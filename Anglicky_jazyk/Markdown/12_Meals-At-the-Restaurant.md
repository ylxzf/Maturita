## Czech Meals

### Breakfast (6-8am weekdays)
- **Typical foods**:
  - Bread/rolls with butter, cheese, ham, jam
  - Cereals: cornflakes, muesli, porridge
  - Beverages: tea, milk, cocoa, coffee

### Lunch (Noon)
- **Structure**:
  - Soup (vegetable/broth)
  - Main dish (meat/fish with sides or sweet dish)
  - Possible dessert
- **Common soups**: Potato, tomato, tripe, goulash
- **Main dishes**:
  - Meat: Beef, pork, veal (boiled/fried/roasted)
  - Sides: Dumplings, potatoes, rice
  - Sweet options: Fruit dumplings, pancakes

### Supper (Evening)
- **Options**:
  - Cold: Bread with spreads
  - Warm: Simple cooked dishes

### Traditional Czech Cuisine
- **Iconic dishes**:
  - Roasted sirloin with cream sauce
  - Pork with sauerkraut
  - Fried cheese
  - Fruit dumplings
- **Note**: Often calorie-dense

## British Meals Comparison

### Breakfast
- **Weekdays**: Cereals, toast, tea/coffee
- **Weekend "Full English"**:
  - Eggs (fried/scrambled)
  - Bacon, sausages, beans
  - Mushrooms, tomatoes

### Lunch
- Typically cold (sandwiches)

### Afternoon Tea (5pm)
- Strong tea with milk
- Biscuits/cakes

### Dinner (6-7pm)
- **Courses**:
  - Soup starter
  - Meat/fish main with vegetables
  - Dessert (fruit/ice cream)
- Beverages: Wine/beer, followed by coffee

## American Food Culture
- **Fast food dominance**: Burgers, fries, hot dogs
- **Convenience foods**: Takeaways, ready meals
- **Popular desserts**: Cheesecake, donuts

## Dining Out

### Restaurant Types
- **Fast food**: McDonald's, KFC
- **Italian**: Pizzerias
- **Cafés**: Confectioneries

### Ordering Process
1. Get seated
2. Drinks order (from menu)
3. Food order (starter, main, dessert)
4. Request bill when finished
5. Optional tipping (10-15% for good service)
